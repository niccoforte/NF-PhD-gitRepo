## Method and selection

Filter the paired macroscopic stress and strain or load-line force and displacement using a causal, second-order, low-pass Butterworth filter, implemented as second-order sections with first-sample DC initialisation. Use the actual saved times to verify sampling, with the existing uniform-grid remapping for timestamp jitter. Apply the same cutoff and timing to both channels. Retain native material initiation and deletion observations. Compute the C(T) endpoint load and load-line work from the paired filtered response at the recorded intact endpoint. Preserve sampling gaps, censored crossings, and invalid negative plastic work as explicit data conditions. The schedule specifies fixed frequencies, with no sample-dependent modal rescaling. The implementation requires a cutoff below the measured Nyquist frequency and an adequately supported history.

The qualification sample comprised 64 saved histories at 10 mm strut length and nominal 20% relative density, with one periodic and seven paired disorder cases per topology and test mode. The sweep compared 22 tested levels per family against the prior archive baseline, the smaller of twice the lowest physical modal frequency and one-sixth of the saved sampling rate. The selected setting was the lowest sampled cutoff that satisfied the sensitivity limits. The baseline and the selected schedule are separate steps. The 2 kHz nominal archive rate and the Abaqus/Explicit pre-output antialiasing convention motivated the baseline. Hsieh et al. applied real-time Butterworth filtering at twice the natural frequency of their SENB specimens. That procedure guides the choice of filter type and provides a reference calculation. These C(T) and UAT cutoffs were selected empirically from native histories for their own fixture and loading conditions. The selection did not use agreement with the digitised Hsieh curve. [Hsieh et al., Section 2.4 and Appendix C](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.01923) · [Abaqus/Explicit output filtering](https://docs.software.vt.edu/abaqusv2025/English/SIMACAEOUTRefMap/simaout-c-dboutput-filter.htm)

The relative tolerances were 2% for peak response, 1% for its opening or strain, and 3% for the 40%-work stiffness estimate, with preservation of observed crossing status. In UAT, the observed 25%-crossing strain and 1%-crossing work each had a 2% tolerance. In C(T), force, opening and work at the same native initiation bracket had tolerances of 1%, 1% and 2%, respectively. These tolerances measure sensitivity to the archive baseline. Late roughness was measured using the RMS of second differences of the sampled response after first deletion when available, or after the raw peak otherwise. The maximum error against the 15 independent elastic stiffness controls was 4.87%. FCC UAT retained the baseline because lower settings moved peak or threshold observations in the screened histories. The limited reduction for hexagonal C(T) reflects the corresponding preservation constraints. ## Independent checks and limits

The latest same-geometry half-rate comparisons place the selected C(T) cutoff at −2.75% in initiation J and −1.38% in equivalent toughness for ordered kagome, and −8.05% in J and −4.11% in equivalent toughness for ordered hexagonal, relative to the slower run at its own recorded last intact sample. These differences combine loading-rate and endpoint effects with post-processing and should remain visible in the limitations. At 225 Hz, the FCC equivalent initiation toughness is approximately 0.61% below the 333.33 Hz result on the same dense original-rate record. This is a filter-sensitivity check, with the geometry, stiffness and failure bracket held fixed. Causal lag and pre-save aliasing remain possible. The procedure qualifies a working archive filter for the sampled families, with additional sampling and fixed-event checks required when transferring the decision beyond that sample.
